Internal Documents Point to Primeval Titan Coming Off the Commander Ban List

Two people with knowledge of the process describe a September update that ends the card's fourteen-year absence. A third source disputes that the list is final.

DK
Dana Kowalczyk Senior Staff Writer · Aug 19, 2026, 11:47 a.m. ET · Updated 12:31 p.m.

Primeval Titan is expected to be removed from the Commander banned list in the format's next quarterly update, according to two people who have seen internal planning documents circulated ahead of the announcement. Both spoke on condition of anonymity because they were not authorized to discuss unreleased decisions.

The six-mana green creature has been banned in Commander since 2012, one of the longest continuous entries on a list that has otherwise turned over substantially in the past two years. Its removal would be the most significant single change since the format's ban list moved under Wizards of the Coast's stewardship and the Commander Format Panel took over recommendations in late 2024.

The documents shared with The Sol Ring Scoop take the form of a working sheet with a column for proposed action and a column for internal notes. The Titan line is marked UNBAN, with a note referencing "power creep at the six-drop slot" and "no measurable win-rate gap in panel testing." The Scoop is publishing a partial view of that sheet with identifying metadata removed.

Why now

Both sources described the reasoning in similar terms. The argument that kept the Titan banned for over a decade — that it fetches two lands per attack and turns any green deck into the same deck — has weakened as land-based value engines became ordinary at the same mana cost, and as the panel's stated philosophy shifted toward removing cards that are merely strong rather than genuinely warping.

The case against it in 2012 was that it made every green deck play the same. Nobody in the room thinks that's true anymore. Person familiar with the panel's discussions

A third person, who has also been briefed on the process, cautioned that the sheet reviewed by The Scoop is a working draft rather than a final list, and said at least one entry has changed since the version we saw. That person would not say which.

The market moved first

Whatever the final list says, the secondary market has already reacted. Listings for the original Magic 2011 printing thinned out over the course of Tuesday, and by that evening several vendors had pulled foil copies from their storefronts entirely — typically a sign that a shop is repricing rather than selling out. One retailer we spoke with said they had moved more copies since Monday night than in the previous eight months combined.

That pattern is familiar. Ahead of the April 2025 update that returned five cards to legality, the same pattern showed up as soon as word began circulating among vendors, well before anything was official. The cards that spiked then were, with one exception, the cards that were unbanned.

What happens if it's real

The practical effect is narrower than the discourse will suggest. Primeval Titan is a six-mana creature that does nothing the turn it lands, in a format where six mana buys a great deal. Its return matters most in decks already built to attack with a threat and untap with lands — which is to say, in the same handful of green shells that already sit at the top of most tables.

It matters more as a signal. If the panel is willing to move on a card that has been a fixture of the list since before the format had a rules document at Wizards, very little on that list is permanent.

Wizards of the Coast did not respond to a request for comment before publication. The Sol Ring Scoop will update this story as it develops.
